# Research: Chapter 1 — What Is AI?

Researched 12 Oct 2026. Only claims that a source supports are listed. Anything marked "own example" is written for the book and is not a sourced claim.

## Findings

### Definition (Section 1.1)
1. **Official definition.** The OECD (2023 update) says an AI system is a machine-based system that, for human-defined explicit or implicit objectives, infers from the input it receives how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments. [S1]
2. **Autonomy varies.** Different AI systems work with different levels of autonomy, and some keep adapting after they are deployed. [S1]
3. **Outputs include "content".** The OECD's wording covers generative AI (text, images, video). [S1]
4. **Plain version.** IBM describes AI as technology that lets computers and machines simulate human learning and related abilities. [S2]
5. **Narrow vs general.** Narrow (weak) AI does a specific task or set of tasks, for example voice assistants. Artificial general intelligence (AGI) would learn and apply knowledge across a wide range of tasks. IBM states AGI is theoretical and that no known system approaches it. [S2]

### History (Section 1.2)
6. **1950.** Alan Turing's paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (*Mind*, 1950) opens with the question "Can machines think?" [S3]
7. **1955–56.** A proposal dated 31 Aug 1955 by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester and Shannon asked for a two-month, about ten-person summer study at Dartmouth College in 1956. It uses the term "artificial intelligence". Its basis was that intelligence can be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it. [S4]
8. **Funding slumps ("AI winters").** Wikipedia's AI winter article dates the first winter to about 1974–1980, after the 1973 Lighthill report in the UK and DARPA's cuts to academic AI research. A second slump ran from 1987 after the LISP machine market collapsed (the article gives 1987–2000). Interest in neural networks revived in the mid-1980s. [S6]
8a. **Deep Blue.** In May 1997, IBM's Deep Blue beat world chess champion Garry Kasparov 3.5–2.5 in a six-game match, the first computer win over a reigning champion under standard tournament conditions. [S8]
8b. **2012.** AlexNet's win in the ImageNet contest helped start the machine-learning boom. [S6]
8c. **30 Nov 2022.** OpenAI released ChatGPT. It reached 1 million users in five days and 100 million monthly users in about two months. [S9]
9. **2017.** The paper "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., submitted 12 Jun 2017) introduced the Transformer, based solely on attention, with no recurrence or convolutions. [S5]

### AI, machine learning and deep learning (Section 1.3)
10. **Nesting.** AI is the broadest field. Machine learning is a subset of AI. Deep learning is a subfield of machine learning, built on neural networks. [S7]
11. **Deep vs classic ML.** Deep learning automates much of feature extraction, can use raw unstructured data (text, images), does not always need labelled data, and needs more data to reach high accuracy. [S7]
