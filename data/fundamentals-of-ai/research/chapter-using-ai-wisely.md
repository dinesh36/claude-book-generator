# Research: Chapter 4 — Using AI Wisely

Researched 12 Oct 2026. Only claims a source supports are listed. Practical advice written by the book is labelled as such.

## Findings

### 4.1 Bias, privacy and safety
1. IBM defines AI bias as biased results caused by human biases that skew the original training data or the AI algorithm. [S1]
2. Causes: unrepresentative or incomplete training data (sample/selection bias), left-out data (exclusion bias), poor problem framing in the algorithm, and human bias in labelling. [S1]
3. Example impacts: harm to marginalised groups in hiring, policing and credit scoring. [S1]
4. Privacy risks: training sets can contain sensitive data (health, finance, biometric); people may not know their data is collected; data shared for one purpose may be reused to train AI; models can leak one user's private information to others. [S2]
5. Basic protections: limit collection, get consent, use encryption and access controls, restrict sensitive domains, and report openly how data is used. [S2]
6. NIST released AI RMF 1.0 on 26 Jan 2023: a voluntary framework for managing AI risks to people, organisations and society. [S3]
7. Stanford's AI Index 2026 reports 362 documented AI incidents in the latest year, up from 233 in 2024. [S4]

### 4.2 Checking AI answers
8. Models can produce false or misleading information that sounds plausible (hallucination). [S5]
9. Researchers argue training and evaluation reward confident guesses over admitting uncertainty. [S6]
10. The checklist in the book (verify numbers, ask for sources and open them, cross-check, take extra care with health/money/law) is the book's own practical advice, based on findings 8–9. It is not quoted from a source.

### 4.3 Where AI is heading
11. AI Index 2026: organisational AI adoption reached 88%; generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years; agent task success on OSWorld rose from 12% to about 66%. [S4]
12. AI Index 2026: 73% of experts expect a positive impact on jobs, versus 23% of the public. [S4]
13. EU AI Act: in force 1 Aug 2024. Risk-based: unacceptable risk (banned, such as social scoring), high risk (most obligations), limited risk (transparency duties, such as telling people they are talking to AI), minimal risk (not regulated). Transparency obligations (Article 50) apply from 2 Aug 2026. [S7]
